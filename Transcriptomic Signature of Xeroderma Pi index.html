EXTENDED ABSTRACT

INTRODUCTION

Xeroderma Pigmentosum (XP) is a rare autosomal recessive genodermatosis caused by defects in the nucleotide excision repair (NER) pathway, leading to extreme ultraviolet hypersensitivity. Clinical manifestations include progressive cutaneous, ocular, and neurological deterioration, along with a more than 10,000-fold increased risk of early skin cancers. Although genes such as XPA through XPG and XPV are known to be involved, molecular diagnosis remains challenging due to extensive clinical and genetic heterogeneity. RNA-seq transcriptomic data combined with artificial intelligence offer a promising avenue for identifying robust molecular biomarkers. Among AI approaches, autoencoders excel at unsupervised dimensionality reduction and can learn latent representations capable of flagging anomalous expression patterns.

OBJECTIVES

To develop and validate autoencoder-based computational models that identify transcriptomic signatures associated with XP, detect aberrant gene expression patterns, and evaluate their utility as an auxiliary molecular diagnostic tool.

METHODOLOGY

Public RNA-seq data from fibroblasts of healthy donors and patients with different XP subtypes were obtained from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). After quality control, genes with zero variance were removed and expression values were normalized to the [0,1] interval. The training set consisted of a fraction of the control samples exclusively, while the remaining control samples and all XP samples formed the test set.

A deep autoencoder was implemented in PyTorch. The encoder comprised three dense layers that compressed the input into a 32-neuron latent space, followed by a symmetric decoder. ReLU activation was used in the intermediate layers and sigmoid in the output. Training employed mean squared error (MSE) loss, the Adam optimizer, and early stopping to prevent overfitting.

Anomaly detection relied on the reconstruction error of each sample: any test sample whose error exceeded a statistically defined threshold was flagged as having a pathological profile. Biological relevance of individual genes was estimated by comparing the mean per-gene reconstruction error between groups. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques were later applied to interpret the latent features driving the classification.

EXPECTED RESULTS

We anticipate identifying a robust transcriptomic signature for XP, enriched in genes related to DNA repair, oxidative stress, and cell-cycle control. The latent representation learned by the autoencoder should yield a consistent separation between control and patient samples, enabling high-sensitivity detection of aberrant profiles. Furthermore, the model may function as a classifier by using both reconstruction error and latent vectors as discriminative descriptors. The XAI-guided interpretation is expected to pinpoint candidate genes as relevant biomarkers for early XP diagnosis, contributing to future translational applications in precision medicine and omics sciences.

FUTURE PERSPECTIVES

We plan to refine the autoencoder architecture by exploring denoising and variational autoencoders (VAEs) to increase the robustness of anomaly detection. Independent cohort validation and integration of multi-omics data (e.g., proteomics, methylation) are also planned to enhance biological interpretability. Ultimately, the optimized model and the list of biomarker genes will be made publicly available on our research group’s website, enabling the scientific community to reproduce and apply the tool for Xeroderma Pigmentosum molecular diagnosis.

EXTENDED ABSTRACT

INTRODUCTION

Melanoma is characterized by substantial molecular heterogeneity, motivating the development of computational approaches capable of identifying disease-associated transcriptomic alterations. One-class anomaly detection is particularly relevant in this context because models can learn the molecular profile of healthy tissue and subsequently identify deviations from this reference without requiring tumor samples during training. Although deep learning approaches such as autoencoders provide flexible representations of high-dimensional transcriptomic data, classical anomaly-detection algorithms may offer competitive performance when sample sizes are limited. Therefore, comparisons across independent cohorts are essential to determine whether increasingly complex models provide meaningful advantages and whether the molecular signals detected are biologically reproducible.

OBJECTIVES

To develop and evaluate a one-class computational framework for detecting melanoma-associated transcriptomic alterations; compare autoencoder performance with classical anomaly-detection methods across independent cohorts; assess external generalization in a frozen test cohort; investigate differences between primary and metastatic melanoma; and evaluate the cross-cohort reproducibility and functional relevance of the identified molecular alterations.

METHODOLOGY

Transcriptomic microarray datasets from six independent Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) cohorts were analyzed: GSE3189, GSE46517, GSE15605, GSE100050, GSE4587, and GSE7553. The datasets included healthy controls, nevi, primary melanoma, and metastatic melanoma samples generated on GPL96 and GPL570 platforms. GSE7553 was reserved as a frozen independent external test cohort.

The computational workflow was designed under a one-class strategy in which model development relied exclusively on healthy controls. Probe-to-gene annotation, cross-study gene harmonization, train-only feature selection, train-fitted normalization, and batch-effect assessment were incorporated to minimize information leakage. Test samples were excluded from feature selection, normalization fitting, model fitting, and threshold definition.

Four anomaly-detection approaches were evaluated: a deep autoencoder, One-Class Support Vector Machine (OC-SVM), Isolation Forest, and PCA-Mahalanobis distance. Model performance was investigated through cross-study evaluation and subsequent external validation in GSE7553. ROC-AUC, AUPRC, balanced accuracy, and complementary classification metrics were used to characterize discrimination. External performance was additionally stratified into primary and metastatic melanoma.

To investigate biological reproducibility, cross-cohort differential expression analyses were performed and consistently altered genes were compared across independent cohorts. Functional enrichment analysis was subsequently applied to reproducibly altered genes to identify biological processes associated with the detected transcriptomic signals.

RESULTS

All one-class approaches except PCA-Mahalanobis demonstrated substantial discriminatory ability in the frozen external cohort GSE7553. OC-SVM achieved the highest external performance, with a ROC-AUC of 0.982 and balanced accuracy of 0.920, outperforming the autoencoder (ROC-AUC = 0.915) and Isolation Forest (ROC-AUC = 0.871). PCA-Mahalanobis showed substantially lower external discrimination (ROC-AUC = 0.371).

Stage-stratified analysis revealed differences in performance according to melanoma stage. For primary melanoma, the autoencoder achieved a ROC-AUC of 0.786, whereas OC-SVM reached 0.929. Performance increased for metastatic melanoma, reaching 0.975 for the autoencoder and 1.000 for OC-SVM. These findings indicate that transcriptomic deviations were more readily discriminated in metastatic than in primary disease.

Cross-cohort molecular analysis identified reproducible alterations involving PRAME, S100B, MITF, CDK2, and BIRC5. Consistent positive differential-expression signals were observed across independent cohorts, supporting the reproducibility of these melanoma-associated molecular alterations. Functional enrichment further highlighted interferon-gamma response, TNF-alpha signaling via NF-kB, inflammatory response, and epithelial–mesenchymal transition, connecting the computationally detected signals to immune, inflammatory, and tumor-progression-related biological processes.

CONCLUSION

One-class anomaly-detection approaches successfully identified melanoma-associated transcriptomic deviations and generalized to an independent external cohort. In this limited-sample transcriptomic setting, OC-SVM outperformed the autoencoder, achieving the strongest external discrimination. The stage-dependent results further demonstrated that primary melanoma represents a more challenging detection scenario than metastatic disease.

FUTURE PERSPECTIVES

Future work should expand validation to additional independent cohorts and sequencing technologies, particularly RNA-seq, to evaluate robustness across platforms and populations. Larger healthy-control datasets may improve estimation of the normal transcriptomic space and enable more extensive optimization of one-class models. Additional strategies may include alternative representation-learning architectures, integration of clinical variables and multi-omics data, and deeper investigation of reproducible molecular signals as potential biomarkers.
